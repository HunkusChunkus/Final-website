Plant Profiles

Meet six native plants. Each profile tells you where the plant grows best and one tip for looking after it.

Kōwhai

Sophora

A small tree with drooping yellow flowers in spring. Tūī and korimako (bellbirds) love the nectar.

  • Where it grows best: Full sun and soil that drains well.
  • Care tip: Plant it somewhere sunny and do not let the roots sit in soggy soil.

Harakeke (New Zealand Flax)

Phormium tenax

A tough plant with tall fans of long, strappy leaves. The leaves are used for weaving, and the flowers feed tūī.

  • Where it grows best: Sun or part shade. It copes with damp or dry soil.
  • Care tip: When cutting leaves, take them from the outside of the fan and leave the centre shoot alone.

Pōhutukawa

Metrosideros excelsa

A coastal tree covered in red flowers in early summer. It is often called the New Zealand Christmas tree.

  • Where it grows best: Full sun. It handles salty wind, so it is great near the sea.
  • Care tip: Watch for myrtle rust, a fungus that makes bright yellow powder on new leaves. If you see it, do not touch it, and report it to the Ministry for Primary Industries.

Tī Kōuka (Cabbage Tree)

Cordyline australis

A tree with a spiky tuft of long leaves on top of a single trunk, with sweet-smelling white flowers in late spring.

  • Where it grows best: Full sun. It grows in most soils and handles wet ground and frost.
  • Care tip: It is very tough. Just keep weeds away from young plants until they are established.

Kawakawa

Piper excelsum

A shrub with heart-shaped leaves. Holes in the leaves are normal because caterpillars like to eat them.

  • Where it grows best: Shade or part shade, in a damp and sheltered spot.
  • Care tip: Protect young plants from frost and keep the soil damp.

Ponga (Silver Fern)

Cyathea dealbata

A tree fern with fronds that are silver-white underneath. The silver fern is a well-known symbol of New Zealand.

  • Where it grows best: Shade, damp soil and shelter from strong wind.
  • Care tip: Water it during dry spells and plant it out of the wind.
